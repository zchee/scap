# W3.0 — the readdir-floor go/no-go gate

The Phase-3 entry gate, recorded here rather than re-measured. Plan §6 fixes
that: "`D_wall` is frozen and is NOT re-measured on the W2b.1 tree … the
`docs/benchmarks/…-phase-3-gate.md` record is **written at Phase 3 from these
frozen numbers**, not from a fresh measurement." Every figure below is quoted
from `docs/benchmarks/2026-08-28-baseline.md`, whose line numbers are given
so each one can be checked against its row rather than against this summary.

The gate was **passed on 2026-08-29** on the W0.2 quiet numbers. This document
exists so the decision that admitted Phase 3 is auditable next to the phase it
admitted; nothing here was decided by the Phase-3 gate lane.

## The rule, as written

> B1/B2 proceeds iff the winning variant's median wall at its `N*` beats
> `D_wall` (B3 at B3's own `N*`, frozen in W0.6) with non-overlapping IQRs (or
> a 95 % bootstrap interval of the difference excluding zero) on corpus a′
> **and** on a+b, and `sys(N*) ≤ 1.5 × sys(1)`.

`D_wall` is the fallback the gate is measured against: `b3-jwalk`, the B3 arm
that would have shipped had the gate failed, at its own `N*` = 4.

## How it was composed — deviation D-2

The gate answers exactly one question: *a new walker, or jwalk?* It is
therefore evaluated on the **best member of the raw-reader family on the
shared scheduler**, `b1-deque`. Only once that question is answered does
Decision B's separate adoption rule pick which reader ships. Evaluating the
gate on `b2-rustix` instead would have conflated the two questions and, on a′,
would have answered the first one with the second one's noise.

## The gate rows (baseline doc lines 117-120, 333-371)

| Corpus | `D_wall` (`b3-jwalk` @ `N*`=4) | `b1-deque` @ 4 | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a′ | 20.32 ms (IQR 19.70-22.18) | 18.91 ms, CI [-2.46, -0.56] | **PASS** |
| a+b | 172.54 ms (IQR 166.25-180.47) | 119.87 ms, CI [-59.05, -47.88] | **PASS** |

Both bootstrap intervals exclude zero, so the disjointness clause is satisfied
on its confidence-interval arm on both corpora.

**Sys clause** (baseline line 396): `sys(N*)` = 431.63 ms against the
`1.5 × sys(1)` ceiling of 490.45 ms (`sys(1)` = 326.97) — holds.

## Which reader ships — the B2 adoption rule (baseline line 427)

Decision B adopts B2 iff `CPU(B2) ≤ 1.05 × CPU(B1)` on a+b at `N*`:

| Reader | CPU @ `N*`=4, a+b | user | sys | wall |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `b1-deque` (B1, `unsafe`) | 457.9 ms | 26.31 | 431.63 | 119.87 |
| `b2-rustix` (B2, safe) | 464.9 ms | 34.04 | 430.84 | 121.79 |

464.9 / 457.9 = **1.015 ≤ 1.05**, so the safe `rustix::fs::Dir` reader ships
and **no `unsafe` enters the crate at all** — ADR-11's scope becomes empty.
The price is the owned `CString` rustix builds per entry: 34.04 − 26.31 =
**7.73 ms** of user CPU over 204,800 entries, inside the 4-8 ms the plan
predicted before measuring.

## Why `b2-rustix` is not the arm the gate was read on

Taken alone, `b2-rustix` on a′ reads 19.66 ms against `D_wall`'s 20.32 with a
CI of [-1.66, +0.13] — a **0.13 ms** overlap. a′ is a weak discriminator by
construction: 2,036 directories whose ~20 ms run is dominated by fixed cost
(process start, 841 repository checks, output) rather than by traversal. a+b,
at 21,360 directories, is the corpus that discriminates, and there
`b2-rustix`'s own CI is [-57.16, -45.95]. D-2 records the reasoning in full.

## `N*` = 4, and the arms that lost

`N*` = 4 for **every one of the six variants**. On `b1-deque` (baseline lines
333-338, 396): wall 119.87 ≤ 131.85 (1.10 × the best) and sys 431.63 ≤ 490.45.
N = 1 and N = 2 fail the wall clause (346.28 and 198.47 ms); N = 8 passes the
wall clause at 130.63 but fails the sys clause at 921.52 against 490.45.

**B4 rejected.** Against `b1-deque` — the one variant sharing its scheduler, so
the comparison isolates the reader — `b4-attrbulk` at `N*` = 4 on a+b shows
*lower* user CPU (22.73 vs 26.31 ms) but **2.16×** the system time (930.60 vs
431.63) and roughly double the wall (243.71 vs 119.87). CORRECTNESS.md §6's
hypothesis — that asking `getattrlistbulk` for `ATTR_CMN_OBJTYPE` forces vnode
resolution and costs kernel time — is confirmed.

**The scheduler A/B is indistinguishable.** H1 and H2 are both refuted, so no
arm wins on evidence and the pool keeps the plan's stated default: per-thread
deques with oldest-end stealing. That is also the scheduler `b2-rustix` was
measured on, so the shipped configuration is the measured one rather than an
extrapolation across arms.

## What the gate did not decide

The gate admitted the B2 path; it did not choose how the walker detects
`.git`. W0.2 measured only open-and-scan and the shipped jwalk walker only
stat-first, so the two were never compared directly. Deviation **D-6** made
that comparison a Phase-3 obligation (**W3.0b**), and it is settled in
`docs/benchmarks/2026-08-29-phase-3.md`, not here.
